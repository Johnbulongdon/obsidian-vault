# Friends beta survey — 2026-05-20

Survey source: UntilFire Research Survey Google Forms analytics export.

## Audience snapshot

10 responses.

Age range:

- 25–34: 70%
- 18–24: 20%
- 35–44: 10%

Current situation:

- Salaried employee: 50%
- Student: 40%
- Freelancer: 10%

Approximate annual income:

- Under $25k: 40%
- $100k–$205k: 40%
- Prefer not to say: 10%
- $25k–$50k: 10%

Country/region responses were messy/freeform but skewed international, including Canada, China, Taiwan, US/USA/United States, and Hong Kong/hk.

## Financial behavior

How often they think about financial independence / retiring early:

- Weekly: 70%
- Monthly: 20%
- Rarely: 10%

Current financial goal:

- Increase income: 40%
- Save more: 20%
- Invest better: 20%
- Escape paycheck-to-paycheck: 10%
- Early retirement/FIRE: 10%

How they track finances:

- Banking app: 9/10
- Notes app: 2/10
- Dedicated finance app: 2/10
- Don’t track consistently: 2/10
- Notion: 1/10
- Spreadsheet: 0/10

## Frustrations in users’ words

Themes from open responses:

- No single/uniform app to manage money centrally.
- USD/CNY exchange rate.
- Investment uncertainty.
- Bank apps show spending categories, but users still cannot see whether the categorization is accurate or whether there is enough money left for future spending.
- Users can save and budget but still do not feel financially independent.
- Bank fees.
- Keeping track of investments.
- Not knowing where to invest.

## FIRE / planning pain points

Which feel hardest:

- Understanding investing: 7/10
- Increasing income: 7/10
- Tracking investments: 5/10
- Budgeting consistently: 4/10
- Staying disciplined: 3/10
- Knowing how much is enough: 2/10
- Fear of market crashes: 2/10
- Comparing progress to others: 1/10

## Previous FIRE tool usage

Have users tried a FIRE calculator or planning tool?

- No: 100%

This is important: early friends/family respondents are not existing FIRE-tool power users. They may need more beginner explanation and less FIRE jargon.

## What would make a financial planning app useful

Open responses:

- Tell me what to do optimally with my current situation.
- Bookkeeping function.
- Investment/tax advice.
- Tailored investing advice.
- Warn me about fees ahead of time.
- Amalgamate investments from different sources.
- Auto-link each bill paid.

## Most valuable product direction

Which would be most valuable:

- AI financial coach: 40%
- Investment projections: 30%
- Personalized FIRE roadmap: 20%
- Spending analysis: 10%

No visible responses selected net worth tracking, habit/accountability system, side-income planning, or community/challenges.

## Simplicity vs depth

Scale result:

- 1: 0%
- 2: 30%
- 3: 20%
- 4: 40%
- 5: 10%

Interpretation: users lean toward wanting depth, but not at the cost of confusion. The center of gravity is 4/5, not pure simplicity.

## Willingness to pay

Would pay for a tool that clearly improved financial planning:

- Maybe: 60%
- Yes, under $5/month: 40%
- No: 0%
- $5–$15/month: 0%
- $15+/month: 0%

Interpretation: willingness exists, but price sensitivity is high. Pro should feel earned and probably start with a low/introductory or free-lifetime early supporter path.

## High-signal open responses

If one financial problem could magically be solved:

- Become super rich.
- USD/CNY back to 7.2.
- Get 1 billion dollar without consequences.
- Investment uncertainty.
- Clear my credit card debt.
- Bank fees.

Follow-up availability:

- Yes: 56.6%
- No: 44.4%

## Product implications

1. Lead less with “FIRE calculator” and more with practical guidance: “what should I do next with my current situation?”
2. The strongest demand is for coaching, investing confidence, and projections, not generic tracking.
3. Investment uncertainty and income growth matter more than pure budgeting.
4. Centralizing financial picture is valuable, but trust and low-friction onboarding matter because users currently rely mostly on banking apps.
5. Since 100% have not used FIRE tools before, explain FIRE concepts gently and avoid assuming prior knowledge.
6. Pricing should stay soft during beta. Early supporters can be rewarded with free Pro/lifetime access.
7. The product should connect the freedom date to specific next moves, especially investing, income, fees, and bills.

## Recommended next product questions

- After seeing your freedom date, do you want investment guidance, income guidance, or spending guidance first?
- What would make you trust an AI financial coach?
- Would you prefer a monthly plan, weekly nudges, or a one-time roadmap?
- Which data would you be willing to enter manually before connecting accounts?
- What would feel scary or too invasive?
