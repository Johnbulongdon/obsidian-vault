# Friends beta survey — 2026-05-20

Survey source: uploaded `UntilFire Research Survey .csv.zip` export from Google Forms.

Latest readout: 25 responses from the CSV export reviewed on 2026-05-28. Treat findings as directional because this is still a small friends/family-style sample.

## Executive summary

- The strongest signal is still not “I need another FIRE calculator.” It is “help me decide what to do next with my real situation.”
- The audience is younger and earlier-stage than a classic FIRE-tool audience: 72% are age 25–34, 24% are students, and 80% have never tried a FIRE calculator or planning tool.
- Money stress clusters around investing confidence, income growth, discipline, and investment tracking more than pure budgeting.
- Banking apps are already the default tracking layer, but they do not answer “am I on track?”, “what should I fix this month?”, or “how do I invest with confidence?”
- The best product wedge is a calm adviser that turns a freedom-date result into an actionable roadmap, with simple UX and practical guidance.
- Price sensitivity is real, but willingness to pay is a little stronger than the earlier 19-response readout: sub-$5/month is still the clearest signal, while a small $5–15/month segment now appears.

## Financial behavior

How often they think about financial independence / retiring early:

- Weekly: 14/25, 56%
- Daily: 5/25, 20%
- Monthly: 4/25, 16%
- Rarely: 2/25, 8%

This means 76% think about it weekly or daily.

Current financial goals skew toward:

- Increase income: 7/25, 28%
- Early retirement / FIRE: 4/25, 16%
- Build side income: 4/25, 16%
- Escape paycheck-to-paycheck: 3/25, 12%
- Save more: 3/25, 12%
- Invest better: 3/25, 12%
- Find a job: 1/25, 4%

Interpretation: FIRE is a meaningful aspiration, but the immediate user job is often “help me earn more, get more stable, and make smarter money decisions.”

How they track finances:

- Banking app: 18/25, 72%
- I don’t track consistently: 7/25, 28%
- Spreadsheet: 5/25, 20%
- Notes app: 4/25, 16%
- Dedicated finance app: 3/25, 12%
- Notion: 1/25, 4%
- Custom self-built tool: 1/25, 4%

Takeaway: UntilFire should not try to win first as a generic ledger. Users already have fragmented tracking. The real job is converting scattered data into a clear plan and next move.

## Frustrations in users’ words

Themes from open responses:

- Bank fees and avoidable charges
- Investment uncertainty and too many choices
- Weak visibility into where money actually goes
- Cross-border account complexity
- Currency volatility, especially USD/CNY
- Rising cost of living, housing, and lifestyle spend
- Subscription/autopay creep
- Tax complexity, including country-specific investment tax pain
- Lack of a single place to manage income, investing, and budget

Representative non-PII quotes:

- “just tell me what to do optimally with my current situation.”
- “Very very simple UX with clean UI and dumbed down features.”
- “If it could amalgamate investments from different sources.”
- “Warning me about the fees ahead of time.”
- “I don't know what's going on with my spending, what plans should I have.”
- “The irish tax codes make investing very complicated.”

- Understanding investing: 16/25, 64%
- Increasing income: 13/25, 52%
- Staying disciplined: 10/25, 40%
- Tracking investments: 9/25, 36%
- Budgeting consistently: 8/25, 32%
- Fear of market crashes: 5/25, 20%

Interpretation: the product should connect the freedom date to investing confidence and practical money moves, not just expense categorization.

## Previous FIRE tool usage

Have users tried a FIRE calculator or planning tool? (24 answered)

- No: 20/24, 83.3%
- Yes regularly: 2/24, 8.3%
- Tried once or twice: 2/24, 8.3%

This is important: early respondents are mostly not existing FIRE-tool power users. They need beginner-safe language, trust signals, and less jargon.

## What would make a financial planning app useful

Open responses point to:

- Clear “what should I do next?” guidance
- Investment and tax advice
- Bookkeeping or budget-left-this-week/month visibility
- Bill linking and autopay visibility
- Fee warnings before problems happen
- Aggregated investments from different sources
- Savings management
- Emergency-fund awareness alongside investing suggestions
- Clean, simple UX with dumbed-down defaults
- All-in-one planning across income, investing, and monthly budget

## Most valuable product direction

Which would be most valuable? (24 answered)

- AI financial coach: 6/24, 25%
- Personalized FIRE roadmap: 5/24, 20.8%
- Investment projections: 5/24, 20.8%
- Spending analysis: 4/24, 16.7%
- Habit/accountability system: 2/24, 8.3%
- Net worth tracking: 1/24, 4.2%
- Side-income planning: 1/24, 4.2%

Interpretation: the winning direction is still an adviser-shaped product. The user does not only want dashboards; they want guidance, projections, and a roadmap in one simple flow.

## Simplicity vs depth

Scale result from 24 responses:

- 2: 7/24, 29.2%
- 3: 6/24, 25%
- 4: 7/24, 29.2%
- 5: 4/24, 16.7%
- 1: 0

Interpretation: users do not want a toy, but they do want a simple surface. Practical depth should live behind clear defaults and friendly explanations.

## Willingness to pay

Would pay for a tool that clearly improved financial planning:

- Yes, under $5/month: 9/25, 36%
- Maybe: 8/25, 32%
- Yes, $5–15/month: 4/25, 16%
- No: 3/25, 12%
- Yes, $15+/month: 1/25, 4%

Interpretation: this sample is still price-sensitive, but not totally low-value. There is some evidence for a light paid tier in the $5–15 range if the value is tangible and trust is earned after the free result.

## High-signal open responses

If one financial problem could magically be solved:

- Increase income or wealth accumulation
- Save more despite cost-of-living pressure
- Track autopays and reduce unnecessary spending
- Resolve investment uncertainty
- Build a clear investment or wealth-management plan
- Handle major family or housing costs
- Create a cleaner monthly split between budget and investing
- Beat inflation after tax drag

Trusted money advice sources/tools are fragmented and weakly held: some mention RocketMoney, SoFi, banking apps, YouTube creators, blogs, DeepSeek, friends/family, or no trusted source at all. There is no dominant trusted planning brand in this sample.

Follow-up availability: 11 yes, 13 no, 1 blank.

## User portfolio readout

The survey points to five practical user clusters:

1. **Investment-confidence seekers** — users who want help understanding where and how to invest.
2. **Income climbers** — users whose main pressure is earning more or building side income.
3. **Global / cross-border planners** — users with fragmented accounts, tax complexity, or currency concerns.
4. **Early-career stabilizers** — students and lower-income respondents who need clarity and discipline before deep FIRE planning.
5. **FIRE-curious planners** — users who do care about retirement freedom, but usually want it translated into a clear path rather than a complex simulator.

## Product implications

1. Lead with “what to do next” and a freedom-date-backed plan, not only a calculator result.
2. Keep the no-login freedom-date result, but make the result screen quickly answer: “Am I on track?” and “What should I do this month?”
3. Beginner FIRE education should be embedded gently in context, not presented as a finance course.
4. The strongest paid value hypothesis is a monthly financial coach/adviser that recommends practical moves across investing, spending, bills/fees, taxes, and income.
5. Keep the UX calm and simple even when the advice engine underneath becomes smarter.
6. Global and tax-aware trust proof matters because the sample already includes cross-border complexity.
7. A higher-price Pro plan still needs stronger proof than this sample provides. Test pricing softly after value.

## Recommended next product questions

- After seeing your freedom date, would you want investing guidance, income guidance, or spending guidance first?
- What would make you trust an AI financial coach with personal finance decisions?
- Which country or tax situations need to feel “accurate enough” before you would rely on it?
- Would you prefer a monthly plan, weekly nudges, or a one-time roadmap?
- Which data would you be willing to enter manually before connecting accounts?
- What would feel scary or too invasive?
- Would you pay for a first personalized monthly FIRE plan if it showed 3 specific actions and their freedom-date impact?
